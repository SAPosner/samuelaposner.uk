Every Mainline Platform of London Waterloo - in a day! {LinkedIn Version}

Hobbies are wonderful things and we must never ignore the skills they help us to develop.

I had a free day last week and set myself a challenge – could I visit all 24 platforms at London Waterloo in a day? ‘A day’ was generous; I had an event in the evening and wanting to avoid peak times meant I really only had seven hours. That makes 17.5 minutes a platform – certainly doable given a trip to Clapham Junction is little more than 10 minutes – but tight.

This was where the planning began. You can (broadly) categorise the platforms at Waterloo into three groups: the low (1-6) numbered platforms are used by ‘metro’ services going out through Wimbledon towards Hampton Court or Guildford; the middle (7-16) ones are used for long-distance services from the South West, Southampton and Portsmouth; and the higher ones (20-24) are for the Windsor-side services, the loops via Kingston and Hounslow, as well as the services onwards to Reading and Windsor itself. The observant amongst you will notice that this leaves 17-19 – I found these were generally used for peak-time extras so needed an extra thought.

The second obstacle was minimising how far I travelled on each train (after all I needed to get back to Waterloo to tick off the platforms). For metro services, Vauxhall was an easy option – if you come from a low-numbered platform and want to return to another low-numbered platform, your train back is just across the island (high to high was again nice and easy): the round trip was doable in ten minutes. For the middle platforms you needed to work a little harder – the first/last stop for most of these services is Clapham Junction but to make space for passengers travelling further you’re not allowed to travel out of Waterloo on these services, only into London – that meant a large number of circulations to tick off these middle platforms, but rather than waste time also presented opportunities to tick off other platforms on the way there.

With all this research gathered, I could begin to plan the day. I quickly figured out the platforms with only a limited service and from there fixed the trains I had to be on in order for the challenge to succeed. Plotting these on a timeline enabled me to see where I had some spare time and when those platforms with a (small) choice of trains could be visited. I again fixed these on the timeline and took stock. The low and high platforms were easier, but a certain amount of jiggling had to be done as clockface frequencies tend to present the same problems over and over again. The whole planning process took several hours and was an exercise in resilience and attention to detail: at moments I thought I had a plan only to discover that it relied in magical teleportation between Vauxhall and Waterloo which I hadn’t quite planned on inventing! By the end of the day before, though, I had my plan and simply needed to cross my fingers and hope the signallers the following day were all on my side!

Thankfully there was very little drama on the day itself – one bit of late running meant I missed a planned connection, but thankfully there was a train about to depart on an adjacent platform which I could catch to save my plan from unravelling. With short connections I quickly picked up the best places in the train to be so I could quickly get up and down the flights of stairs needed to change trains and this certainly helped me stay on track given in the up (towards London) direction trains tended not to wait for their scheduled departure time before leaving. My final train pulled into platform 13 (which in hindsight probably should not have been left to the end) bang on time and I could celebrate a plan coming into fruition. I had spent a lot of time worrying about last-minute platform alterations but equipped with all the platform information I am sure I could have found alternative plans.

The key takeaway for me is the importance of planning but also being ready at a moments’ notice to change your course of action. The delayed train through me as I thought I couldn’t make it back to Vauxhall in time for my next train, but checking the information carefully and positioning myself properly meant this wasn’t a problem at all. This was also an exercise in time management and scheduling with the need to quickly identify the trains which were non-negotiable and building the day around this. In short, hobbies are fun and shape our character in ways we sometimes don’t notice.
